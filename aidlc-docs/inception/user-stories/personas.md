# Course AIdvisor Personas

> **Status:** Generated from the explicitly approved story-generation plan; awaiting review and explicit approval.

These personas represent student archetypes. They are identified by class standing or major rather than personal names to reduce identity-based bias. Academic advisors are supplemental stakeholders who may read the same journey outputs; they do not have a distinct workflow or persona in this story set.

## Persona 1: Incoming Freshman — Information Science, Coding Background

- **Context**: Incoming Cornell Information Science student with some R experience and no Python experience.
- **Goals**: Explore data science coursework, find an appropriate starting point in a programming sequence, and plan the next four semesters while considering Economics and Philosophy minors.
- **Needs**: Conversational intake, a placement quiz when prior knowledge is unclear, course and review information, requirement mapping, a cited plan, and plan refinement.
- **Constraints**: Wants a manageable credit load, has time-of-day preferences, and prefers course difficulty at or below 4.0 where review data is available.
- **Journey**: First-Year Student Builds a Two-Year Plan.

## Persona 2: Junior — Statistics Major Considering an Economics Minor

- **Context**: Cornell junior majoring in Statistics who has completed some coursework toward a current minor and is considering adding an Economics minor.
- **Goals**: Understand remaining program requirements and identify a realistic path to graduation that fits career interests and workload limits.
- **Needs**: Manual completed-course entry, requirement coverage, next-semester options, prerequisite and overlap checks, and explanations grounded in sources.
- **Constraints**: Has a limited number of semesters remaining; planning depends on available catalog and roster data.
- **Journey**: Upperclassman Checks Requirements and Finishes a Minor.

## Persona 3: Undergraduate — Information Science Major and Cognitive Science Minor Exploring Career Direction

- **Context**: Cornell undergraduate majoring in Information Science with a Cognitive Science minor who is unsure whether to pursue research or industry and has not settled on a course direction.
- **Goals**: Explore courses that connect interests and possible career directions without committing prematurely, and understand how options fit academic requirements.
- **Needs**: Follow-up questions about interests and goals, course summaries and reviews, requirement mapping, cited rationale, and an advising handoff for decisions the system cannot verify.
- **Constraints**: Course and program advice must reflect retrieved or curated data; unknown information must be labeled unverified.
- **Journey**: Student Explores a Course and Hits the Edge of the Product, with course exploration feeding future planning.

## Persona 4: Sophomore — Non-Technical, Considering Economics

- **Context**: Cornell sophomore with limited technical coursework who is considering changing majors to Economics and is unsure how a change would affect academic progress.
- **Goals**: Understand what changing to Economics may involve, whether a feasible course path can be outlined from available information, and what steps to take to evaluate the change.
- **Needs**: A clear explanation of represented Economics requirements, questions about completed and planned courses, a comparison of current progress with possible Economics coursework, and guidance on whom to contact for official change-of-major decisions.
- **Constraints**: Has not settled on the major change; feasibility depends on accurate course history, catalog requirements, course availability, and individual college or department rules that may not be represented in the product.
- **Journey**: Explores Courses and Requirements Before Deciding on a Major. The assistant can outline an informational path from represented data, label unknowns, and direct official eligibility or approval questions to Cornell academic advising; it does not decide or guarantee whether the change is possible.

## Persona 5: Senior — ORIE Major Checking Graduation Requirements

- **Context**: Cornell senior majoring in Operations Research and Information Engineering (ORIE), preparing to graduate and checking completed coursework against represented major requirements.
- **Goals**: Identify which represented ORIE requirements appear satisfied or remaining, surface potential gaps or course-matching uncertainties, and prepare focused questions for an academic advisor.
- **Needs**: Manual entry of completed courses, cited mapping to curated ORIE requirements, prerequisite and overlap checks for remaining course options, and a clear summary of what still needs official confirmation.
- **Constraints**: Has limited semesters remaining; requirement substitutions, exceptions, college-specific rules, and official degree-audit determinations may not be available to the assistant.
- **Journey**: Checks ORIE Requirement Coverage and Plans Next Steps Before Graduation. The assistant provides an informational comparison, labels unverified matches, and directs official graduation clearance to Cornell academic advising; it does not issue an official degree audit.
